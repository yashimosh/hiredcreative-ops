# Lead Generation — Filling the Pipeline

**Sources:** Aaron Ross — *Predictable Revenue*; Jeb Blount — *Fanatical Prospecting*; Alex Hormozi — *$100M Leads*; April Dunford (ICP).
**Applied here to job search:** channels and verticals are your target companies/archetypes from `modes/_profile.md`, not a fixed list — customize `portals.yml` for your own targets.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/100m-leads.md`, `1-page-marketing-plan.md`.

## The three lead types (Ross)

- **Seeds** — relationships, referrals, word of mouth. Slowest to grow, highest close rate, near-zero cost. → `library/networking.md`.
- **Nets** — one-to-many: content, portfolio, being findable. Medium everything. → portfolio site, Behance/LinkedIn presence.
- **Spears** — targeted outbound to named companies/roles. Fast, controllable, labor-heavy. → `scan`, `contacto`.
- Rule: never depend on one type. Seeds pay off in quarters; spears pay off in weeks; run spears while seeding.

## The core four (Hormozi)

Warm outreach · cold outreach · content · paid ads (n/a for a job search). Priority order: **warm outreach first** (highest reply rate, zero cost — the lane most candidates skip), cold outbound second, content third (compounds — posting your work publicly).
- Volume math is honest math: define how many first-touches per week the pipeline needs, then count them. Hope is not a channel.
- "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation" — free audit/diagnosis content generates inbound without eroding the paid engagement.

## ICP discipline (Dunford + Baker)

- An ICP is written down or it doesn't exist: segment, size, buying trigger, budget signal, disqualifiers.
- Qualify on **observable signals**, not vibes — hiredcreative-ops encodes this: the A-G evaluation scoring in `modes/oferta.md`. Trust the score; evaluating harder beats applying wider.
- Buying-trigger events outrank static attributes: new funding, new location opening, rebrand-smell (inconsistent naming/visuals), new leadership, event sponsorship, hiring for marketing roles. A mediocre-fit account in a trigger window beats a perfect-fit account at rest.

## Prospecting discipline (Blount)

- **The 30-day rule:** the prospecting you do (or skip) this month is the pipeline you have (or don't) in 90 days. Dry spells are always self-inflicted, always lagged.
- **Time-block it:** prospecting happens in a fixed daily/weekly block, before reactive work. It is never "when there's time" — there is never time.
- **The Law of Replacement:** every deal that closes or dies must be replaced with more than one new prospect, because most prospects don't convert.
- Don't over-research before first touch — go deep on a company only after a signal of life (reply, response, an actual interview). Shallow + broad for discovery (`scan`), deep only post-signal or for your top-tier targets.

## Pipeline math (run monthly, honestly)

```
target # of offers ÷ offer rate    = final-round interviews needed
interviews ÷ interview→final rate  = first interviews needed
interviews ÷ reply→interview rate  = replies needed
replies ÷ reply rate               = applications needed  → per week
```
If the weekly application number is not being hit, nothing downstream is a strategy problem — it's a sending problem: stop over-researching individual companies and start applying.

## List building

- Build lists from **watering holes** (where your target roles/companies already gather): the boards and ATSs in `portals.yml`, industry Slack/Discord communities, alumni networks, LinkedIn "people also viewed."
- Every pipeline entry needs: company, role, channel, signal observed (why this one, why now), source. No signal recorded = not a real lead, just a URL.
- Cap active pursuit to a working set you can actually follow up on well — a long saved list is fine, but the set you're actively pursuing should stay small.

## Anti-patterns

- Researching as procrastination — enrichment beyond what the next application actually needs.
- Mass-blast, generic applications (low reply rate; also reputation-fatal in a small industry where recruiters compare notes).
- New-channel hopping when the current channel's volume was never actually hit.
- Treating inbound-someday (content, a public portfolio) as a substitute for applying today.
