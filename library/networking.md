# Networking — Relationships, Warm Intros, Events

**Sources:** Keith Ferrazzi — *Never Eat Alone*; Adam Grant — *Give and Take*; Mark Granovetter — weak-ties research; Nick Gray — *The 2-Hour Cocktail Party*.
**Data layer:** this fork doesn't ship a dedicated relationship-CRM mode — track contacts however works for you (a simple `data/contacts.md`, a spreadsheet, your existing address book). The methodology below applies regardless of where you log it.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/weak-ties.md`, `how-to-win-friends.md`, `culture-map.md`.

## First principles

- **Give first, give disproportionately** (Ferrazzi/Grant): the ledger is never kept out loud, but generosity compounds into deal flow. Every touch should deliver something — an intro, a link, a spotted opportunity, real praise with specifics.
- **Givers win long-term — with boundaries** (Grant): otherish giving (generous AND self-respecting) outperforms; doormat giving burns out. 5-minute favors are the unit: high value to them, low cost to you.
- **Weak ties carry the opportunities** (Granovetter): jobs and clients come disproportionately from acquaintances, not close friends — close ties know what you know. Systematic weak-tie maintenance (the quarterly ping) is where the ROI hides.
- **Never network from need:** build before the ask exists. Reaching out only when hunting is visible and repels. (Reactivation is still better than silence — do it with a give, not an ask.)
- **The network is a graph, not a list:** super-connectors (who know everyone) matter more than seniors. Track connection paths and tiers, not just names — invest in nodes, not just targets.

## Warm intro mechanics (the highest-value move in this file)

1. Identify the path (target ← connector ← you) — who in your existing network already knows someone at the company you're targeting.
2. Ask the CONNECTOR for permission, not the favor itself: "Do you know {target} well enough to intro me? I want to show them {specific}."
3. Send a **forwardable blurb** — 3 sentences the connector can paste: who you are (one clause), the specific reason for THIS intro, the easy next step. Write it so forwarding costs them 10 seconds.
4. **Double opt-in:** the connector checks with the target first. Never make a connector burn capital on an ambush.
5. Report back the outcome to the connector + thank them regardless of result — connectors who hear outcomes intro again.

## Touch cadence (systematize or it doesn't happen)

| Tier | Who | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Active allies, champions, super-connectors | Monthly touch |
| T2 | Warm, relevant, proven responsive | Quarterly ping |
| T3 | Known, dormant | 2×/year (a seasonal or year-end moment works well as a natural excuse) *(judgment)* |
- A touch = give-first: relevant link, congrats on something specific, an intro, a question only they can answer. "Checking in" is not a touch.
- After ANY meeting/event contact: follow up within 24–48h with one specific reference to the conversation (the follow-up IS the relationship; most people never send it).
- Log touches somewhere durable — unlogged touches don't exist.

## Events (Ferrazzi + Gray)

- **The work is before and after; the event is the middle.** Pre-event: get the attendee/sponsor list if there is one, pick 3–5 named targets, prep one specific line for each.
- Aim for quality: 3 real conversations beat 30 cards. Depth signal: you learned a problem they have.
- Exit lines are a kindness — "I promised myself I'd meet three new people tonight; great talking" keeps you moving without rudeness.
- **Hosting beats attending** (Gray): the host meets everyone, owes no one, and sets the frame. A recurring small gathering (8–15 people, 2 hours, name tags, hard end time) is cheap and makes you the connector node in your local creative scene — the position with compounding returns.
- Speaking/teaching = one-to-many trust: a 20-min talk in your specialty at a local event out-networks a month of DMs.

## Digital presence as passive networking

- Comment thoughtfully on targets' posts BEFORE any DM (familiarity priming — see combo tactics in `library/outreach.md`).
- Your own posting = a give to the whole graph at once (what you're learning/building — demo-first doctrine applies).
- LinkedIn/Instagram profile is the landing page of every touch; it must state the position (`library/positioning.md`) in one line.

## Anti-patterns

- Transactional networking (ask embedded in first contact with a new tie).
- Collecting contacts without logging context — a name without a connection path + notes is dead weight.
- Only maintaining comfortable ties (strong ties feel good, weak ties pay).
- Skipping the connector report-back (kills the intro engine silently).
- Delegating relationship voice entirely to AI — drafts yes, but a detectable-template touch to a T1 costs more than no touch. Keep T1 touches hand-finished.
