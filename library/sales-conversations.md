# Sales Conversations — Discovery, Diagnosis, Trust

**Sources:** Neil Rackham — *SPIN Selling*; Mahan Khalsa — *Let's Get Real or Let's Not Play*; David Maister — *The Trusted Advisor*; Keenan — *Gap Selling*.
**Feeds:** `local prep`, `local proposal`, first client calls, and (adapted) job interviews.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/spin-selling.md`, `lets-get-real.md`, `trusted-advisor.md`, `how-to-win-friends.md`, `culture-map.md`.

## The trust equation (Maister)

```
trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) / self-orientation
```
- The denominator dominates: everything that signals "I need this deal" divides trust. Talking about yourself, rushing to pitch, glossing over misfit — all self-orientation.
- Credibility = specifics + honesty about limits ("that part isn't my craft" builds more trust than claiming everything).
- Reliability = many small kept promises before the big one (send the thing when you said; recap emails same-day).
- Intimacy = safe to tell you the messy truth. Earned by discretion and by naming awkward things gently first.

## Diagnose before you prescribe (Khalsa)

- The client's stated problem is a symptom ~70% of the time. "We need a new logo" usually = revenue/positioning problem wearing a design costume.
- **Move off the solution:** when they lead with a prescription ("we want a website"), ask what it's supposed to cause: "If the site were perfect, what changes for the business?"
- **Evidence → impact chain:** for every problem claim ask (a) how do you see/measure that today? (b) what does it cost you? No evidence + no cost = not a real problem = no budget.
- Get all decision constraints on the table early: who decides, against what alternatives, by when, with what budget shape. "Let's get real" — asking directly is respectful, not pushy.
- Yellow lights (hesitation, vagueness, "we'll see") are explored NOW, not survived until the proposal dies silently.

## SPIN structure for discovery (Rackham)

Sequence, not script:
1. **Situation** — minimum needed; research should have answered most (asking basics you could've Googled burns credibility).
2. **Problem** — where does it hurt: "What's hardest about X right now?"
3. **Implication** — grow the problem's perceived cost: "What does that do to {sales/hiring/donor trust} over a year?" ← the questions that actually sell; spend most time here.
4. **Need-payoff** — let THEM articulate the value: "If that were fixed, what would it be worth?" Their words become your framing for the role's impact (and an anchor for the comp conversation, see `library/negotiation.md`).
- Big-ticket selling ≠ closing tricks. In expertise sales, pushy closing *reduces* win rates; advancing commitment does the work.

## The gap (Keenan)

- Sale size = distance between current state (measured, painful) and future state (vivid, valued). No articulated gap = no urgency = "let me think about it."
- Quantify both ends in their numbers during discovery; the proposal then just documents the gap and prices the bridge.

## Every conversation ends with an advance

- **Advance** = a specific commitment that moves the deal (intro to the decision-maker, data shared, meeting with a date, proposal review call booked).
- **Continuation** = warm words, no commitment ("sounds great, stay in touch") — count it as a stall, follow the cadence in `library/outreach.md`.
- Always propose the next step yourself, with a date. Silence you caused is the only failure.

## Meeting flow (default 45 min)

1. Rapport, brief — in relationship-driven markets/cultures, don't rush this; relationship talk can legitimately be the meeting's first third.
2. Agenda + permission ("I'll ask a lot of questions, then share what I've seen — fair?").
3. Discovery (SPIN core, ~60% of time). Listen ratio ≥ 60/40 them.
4. Show, don't pitch: ONE relevant proof artifact — a portfolio piece, a case study, a demo. Demo-first beats describe-first.
5. Money conversation (range test — never leave an interview without a comp-range signal; see `modes/negotiation.md`).
6. Agree the next step + recap same day in writing.

## Anti-patterns

- Pitching in minute 5. Presenting a deck at all (conversation > presentation — Enns).
- Free consulting mid-conversation: diagnose the WHAT freely, never the HOW.
- Accepting "send me a proposal" without a decision conversation — a proposal into a void is a lottery ticket.
- Interview adaptation note: same machine works for job interviews — discovery about the team's pain, STAR+R stories as proof artifacts, advance = defined next step. See `modes/interview-prep.md`.
