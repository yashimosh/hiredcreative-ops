# Negotiation — Deep Method Behind `modes/negotiation.md`

**Sources:** Chris Voss — *Never Split the Difference*; Fisher & Ury — *Getting to Yes*.
**The operating mode (tactics table, reply drafting, chess doctrine) is `modes/negotiation.md` — read it first. This file is the method it draws on.** Applies to job-offer comp negotiation.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/never-split-the-difference.md`, `culture-map.md` (cross-cultural negotiating style).

## Position vs. interests (Fisher/Ury)

- Positions are what they SAY they want ("20% off", "$70k max"); interests are WHY (budget approval optics, risk fear, internal politics, cash-flow timing). Trade on interests — many are cheap for you to satisfy without touching price/comp.
- **BATNA discipline:** know your true walk-away before replying to anything. Their leverage = your alternatives, not their words. Hired Creative Ops: pipeline breadth IS the BATNA — a full `data/applications.md` pipeline with multiple live offers changes every reply's posture.
- Objective criteria as shield: market rates, the rate card, comparable engagements — arguing from standards feels fair and depersonalizes the "no".

## Voss — tactical empathy toolkit

- **Labels:** name their emotion/position before countering — "Sounds like the budget got squeezed" / "Seems like speed matters more than scope here." Defuses before it's argued.
- **Mirrors:** repeat their last 1–3 key words as a question. Cheapest information-extraction tool; they elaborate, you learn the real interest.
- **Calibrated questions:** open "how/what" questions that hand them the problem — "How am I supposed to do that at that number?" / "What happens if this doesn't get fixed this quarter?" Never "why" (accusatory).
- **Accusation audit:** open by naming what they're probably thinking ("You might be thinking this is high for the local market…") — deflates the objection by owning it first.
- **"No" is safety:** questions that invite "no" move faster than chasing "yes" — "Would it be crazy to…" / "Have you given up on solving X?"
- **"That's right" > "you're right":** summarize their world so accurately they say "that's right" — that's the moment leverage shifts. "You're right" = get-off-my-back.
- **7-38-55 / voice:** on calls, the calm late-night-DJ tone downshifts conflict. In text, sentence length does the same job — short sentences read calm.

## Anchoring & concessions

- Whoever anchors credibly first frames the range; anchor high WITH justification (rate card, comparable, value math from discovery).
- **Never split the difference** — meeting in the middle twice = you've conceded twice. Counter with non-round, justified numbers ($8,700 reads calculated; $9,000 reads haggle-able).
- **Ackerman pattern (adapted):** plan concession steps in advance, decreasing size (e.g., 100% → 92% → 87% → 85% + non-monetary sweetener at the floor). Decreasing steps signal a real floor.
- Every concession is CONDITIONAL ("I can do X if you can do Y") and max ONE per message (`modes/negotiation.md` standing rule). Unconditional concessions teach them to push again.
- Concede scope, ceremony, timing — never rate (clients) / never base (comp). Base-rate cuts reprice ALL future work; scope cuts don't.

## Job-offer comp specifics

- Never give the first number if avoidable; deflect with range-of-role research + "flexible on the mix". If forced: anchor top-of-researched-band + rationale.
- Negotiate the PACKAGE, not the salary line: base, equity, sign-on, remote terms, review-at-6-months, title, learning budget — multiple axes = multiple trades.
- After a verbal offer: enthusiasm + gratitude + "I'd like to review the full picture" — never accept or counter in the same call. 24–48h.
- Competing processes stated honestly and without threat = strongest lever. Fabricated deadlines burn trust permanently.

## Reading the table

- Deadlines: theirs are usually softer than stated; yours (44-day-runway class pressure) must NEVER leak into drafts — urgency reads as weakness and invites grinding.
- The person grinding hardest on price is often not the decision-maker — calibrated question: "Who else weighs in on this?"
- Silence after your counter = working, not rejecting. Do not fill it. Do not send a follow-up concession (the classic self-bid).
- If they reopen agreed points repeatedly: name the pattern once, then require the full package agreed in writing before further moves.
