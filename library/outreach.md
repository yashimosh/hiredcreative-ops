# Outreach — First Touches That Get Replies

**Sources:** Jeb Blount — *Fanatical Prospecting*; Tony Hughes — *Combo Prospecting*; Josh Braun-school cold email craft; Robert Cialdini — *Influence* (reciprocity, social proof).
**Your own overrides:** if you've set voice, geo-framing, or trust-building rules in `modes/_profile.md`, those take precedence over the generic guidance below.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/brain-audit.md`, `how-to-win-friends.md`, `persuasive-technology.md`, `nudge.md`, `influence.md`.

## Anatomy of a first touch

1. **Proof you looked** — one specific, verifiable observation about *them* (their new location, their bilingual menu chaos, their grant announcement). This is the whole differentiator; 30 seconds of their time is bought with evidence of your 10 minutes.
2. **Relevance bridge** — why that observation matters to *their* outcome (not to your service).
3. **Credibility in one clause** — a peer-proof point, not a bio. ("did this for X" > "I have 7 years experience").
4. **One easy CTA** — interest-based, not calendar-grabbing. "Worth a look?" / "Want the one-pager?" beats "Do you have 30 minutes Tuesday?"
- Total: 3–5 sentences. If it scrolls, it fails. First sentence must be about them, never "I".

## Braun-school principles

- **Poke the bear:** lead with a question that surfaces a problem they haven't priced ("How are you handling X when Y?") — curiosity beats claims.
- **Detach from the outcome:** write like someone with a full pipeline. Neediness leaks through words like "just following up", "any chance", "I'd love".
- **Illuminate, don't persuade:** show them something they didn't know about their own situation (the 1-page audit is exactly this weapon).
- Reduce friction: no links in first cold touch on channels that punish them; no attachments unless the attachment IS the gift.

## Multi-channel combos (Hughes)

- One channel = one coin flip. Combos convert: LinkedIn comment on their post + LinkedIn DM + email; or email → a mutual connection's intro → in-person at an event.
- Same-day triple tap for a dream-role hiring manager: engage with their content, then DM, then email referencing both. Familiarity compounds within a day.
- Channel choice is theirs, not yours: some hiring managers live on LinkedIn, some prefer email, some are only reachable through a referral — read `contacto.md`'s target research before picking a channel.

## Follow-up cadence

- Most replies come from touches 2–5; most senders stop after 1. Persistence is professional when every touch adds NEW value (a new observation, a relevant example, a useful link) — never "bumping this".
- Default cadence: T+0 first touch → T+3 new-value follow-up → T+8 different channel → T+15 break-up note ("closing the loop — if timing's wrong, no worries") → recycle to nurture (quarterly).
- Break-up notes get outsized reply rates; write them warm, not passive-aggressive.
- Log every touch somewhere durable — an untracked touch might as well not have happened, since you won't know when to follow up.

## Warm > cold, always

- Before any cold touch, check your own network for a path: a warm intro converts an order of magnitude better than the best cold copy. Ask for intros with a forwardable blurb (see `library/networking.md` §warm intros).
- Reactivation of dormant contacts is some of the highest-ROI outreach that exists: "thinking of you because {specific}" + zero ask, or a direct "here's what I'm doing now, who do you know…".

## Personalization ladder (spend where it pays)

- **Tier 1 (named target, high win-prob):** full research, bespoke observation, possibly a demo artifact (Loom, concept frag). 30–60 min.
- **Tier 2:** template + 2 custom fields (observation + name). 5–10 min.
- **Tier 3 / nurture:** value-drop broadcast (useful content), no ask.
- Never send Tier-3 effort to a Tier-1 target; never spend Tier-1 effort before qualification.

## Anti-patterns (auto-reject any draft containing)

- Opening with "I hope this finds you well" / self-introduction paragraph / company history.
- Feature lists, service menus, more than one CTA, more than one question.
- Flattery without specificity ("love what you're doing!").
- AI-voice tells (em-dash chains, "moreover", triadic flourishes) — run against anti-ai-voice rules.
- Any claim you can't prove in one click (portfolio link must back every stated capability).
- Asking for a call in touch #1 to a cold prospect (KRI exception: WhatsApp voice-note culture makes soft voice asks acceptable at touch #2).
