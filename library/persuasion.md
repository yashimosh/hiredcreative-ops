# Persuasion — The Mechanisms Underneath Every Tactic

**Sources:** Robert Cialdini — *Influence*; Dale Carnegie — *How to Win Friends and Influence People*; B.J. Fogg — *Persuasive Technology*; Thaler & Sunstein — *Nudge*.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/influence.md`, `how-to-win-friends.md`, `persuasive-technology.md`, `nudge.md`.
**Relationship to other files:** this is the psychology layer underneath `outreach.md`, `sales-conversations.md`, and `negotiation.md` — those files tell you WHAT to do; this file explains WHY it works, so you can improvise correctly when a situation doesn't match a script.

## Core thesis

- People rarely decide through cold cost-benefit math. They decide through mental shortcuts (Cialdini's "weapons of influence," Thaler/Sunstein's System 1) that are usually right and exploitable when wrong.
- Every mechanism below is neutral — legitimate when it surfaces something REAL (real scarcity, real expertise, real social proof), manipulative the moment it's manufactured. Sophisticated buyers (NGO procurement, experienced clients) detect fabricated versions and penalize them harder than if you'd said nothing.
- The single highest-leverage move available to a small, reputation-dense operator: BE the thing the mechanism points at — genuinely scarce, genuinely expert, genuinely liked — rather than staging the signal.

## The six + one mechanisms (Cialdini)

| Mechanism | The lever | Legitimate trigger | Manufactured version (avoid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocity** | Unreturned gifts create discomfort | A real, useful, unsolicited value-add before any ask (the 1-page audit) | Fake "exclusive" freebies with hidden strings |
| **Commitment/consistency** | People act consistent with prior stated positions | Get them to state their own problem/goal early (SPIN need-payoff) — they then sell themselves the fix | Manufactured "yes ladders" with trivial agreements |
| **Social proof** | "What are people like me doing" | Real client logos, real testimonials, peer-similar case studies | Fabricated stats, bought reviews |
| **Liking** | We say yes to people we like — similarity, praise, cooperation | Genuine common ground, sincere specific praise (Carnegie) | Generic flattery, fake rapport |
| **Authority** | Deference to perceived expertise/credentials | Demonstrated work, specific expertise signals, real referrals | Borrowed/implied credentials, name-dropping without substance |
| **Scarcity** | Loss aversion around what's rare or closing | Real capacity caps (max 5 active conversations), real deadlines | Fake "only 2 spots left" pressure |
| **Unity** *(if present in your edition)* | "One of us" — shared identity, not just similarity | Genuine shared category (fellow Kurd, fellow founder, fellow creative) | Performed in-group signaling |

## Carnegie's layer — persuasion through dignity, not pressure

- The craving to feel important is the master lever (see `how-to-win-friends.md` in full). Every Cialdini mechanism lands harder when the person also feels genuinely respected and heard.
- Practical fusion: use reciprocity/social proof/authority to open the door; use Carnegie's listening + face-saving + "let them own the idea" to walk through it without triggering resistance.
- The "argue against your own interest" move (state one honest limitation) does double duty: it's Cialdini's authority-through-honesty AND Carnegie's humility — both raise trust simultaneously.

## Fogg's layer — persuasion embedded in the medium itself

- **Credibility gate:** presumed (starts at zero for an unknown solo consultant from Iraq) → surface (does it look competent) → reputed (third-party endorsement) → earned (they experienced your competence directly). You can't buy your way past the gate with better copy — you have to earn reputed/earned credibility through work.
- **Kairos (timing):** the SAME message lands or dies based on when it's sent. Trigger sequences off behavior (a reply, a profile view, a stated pain point, the moment after a favor was received) rather than fixed day-counts.
- **The functional triad:** every touchpoint (site, proposal PDF, an AI agent) is simultaneously a tool (does it work), a medium (does it simulate an experience), and a social actor (does it feel like someone). People will infer a personality from any AI-driven touchpoint whether you designed one or not — author the tone deliberately.

## Nudge's layer — persuasion through structure, not argument

- **Defaults win more than persuasive copy.** Whatever requires zero action from the prospect (a pre-selected proposal tier, a pre-filled form) captures disproportionate share regardless of how good the alternative options are. Audit the default before polishing the pitch.
- **The publicity principle as an ethics gate:** never design a nudge you wouldn't explain out loud to the client. Transparency about why a default was chosen doesn't reduce its effectiveness — there's no trade-off to being upfront.
- Loss-aversion framing ("what you lose by not fixing this") tests stronger than gain framing ("what you gain") for risk-averse buyers — use in implication-stage discovery questions (`sales-conversations.md`).

## Ethical line (the one rule that resolves every edge case)

Ask: **would this still work if the other person could read my notes on why I'm doing it?** If yes — it's honest persuasion (real credibility, real scarcity, genuine interest). If the tactic depends on them NOT knowing the mechanism, it's manipulation. In a market as small and relationship-dense as KRI, manipulation detected once ends the relationship and travels through the network (`weak-ties.md`) faster than in an anonymous market.
