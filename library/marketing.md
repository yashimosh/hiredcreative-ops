# Marketing — The System Behind the Pipeline

**Sources:** Allan Dib — *The 1-Page Marketing Plan*; April Dunford — *Obviously Awesome*; Alex Hormozi — *$100M Leads*; Sean D'Souza — *The Brain Audit*.
**Deep sources (full distillations):** `library/custom/books/1-page-marketing-plan.md`, `obviously-awesome.md`, `100m-leads.md`, `brain-audit.md`.
**Relationship to other files:** `lead-generation.md` is about filling the pipeline with prospects (tactics, channels, volume math). This file is one level up — the SYSTEM that produces a repeatable flow of prospects, converts them, and compounds value from existing clients. `positioning.md` feeds the message this system carries.

## Core thesis

- Marketing is a system with three phases (before/during/after a sale), not a single activity like "posting on Instagram" or "sending cold DMs." A gap in any phase caps the whole system's output regardless of how good the other phases are.
- The message only works when it's built on real positioning (`positioning.md`) — a marketing system amplifies whatever message it's given, including a weak one.
- For a solo operator, the constraint is never ideas — it's a coherent, written system that runs without being reinvented weekly.

## The 9-square canvas (Dib) — before / during / after

**BEFORE (turning strangers into prospects):**
1. **Target market** — narrow enough that a message can speak to one person, not a demographic. Score candidate niches on Personal-fulfillment × Value × Profitability (PVP) — pick the niche that wins on all three, not just the biggest.
2. **Message** — a sharp USP built from what makes you different (feeds directly from `positioning.md`'s competitive-alternatives work) + an elevator pitch that names the specific problem solved for the specific segment.
3. **Media** — the channel(s) that reach that specific target market where they already gather (same "watering holes" logic as `lead-generation.md`).

**DURING (turning prospects into leads, then leads into customers):**
4. **Lead capture** — an "ethical bribe" (free diagnostic, mini-guide, the 1-page audit) that converts the ~40% who are interested-but-not-ready, not just the ~3% who are ready today. Without this, marketing only ever catches the most impatient sliver of the market.
5. **Lead nurture** — a cadence of useful contact for everyone who isn't ready yet (maps to `outreach.md`'s follow-up cadence + `library/networking.md`'s touch tiers) — "farming, not hunting": consistent small deposits, not one big ask.
6. **Sales conversion** — trust-based selling, not pressure (feeds from `sales-conversations.md`).

**AFTER (turning a hire into a compounding asset — less directly applicable to a one-time job search, included for completeness):**
7. **Deliver a world-class experience** — the thing that generates case studies and referrals; for a job search, this is simply doing the job well once hired.
8. **Increase lifetime value** — for an ongoing practice this means retainers/repeat engagements; for a career, the equivalent is promotions and expanded scope at the same employer.
9. **Orchestrate and stimulate referrals** — ask people you've worked well with for a specific reference or LinkedIn recommendation; don't leave it to chance.

## Positioning as the marketing multiplier (Dunford)

- Marketing amplifies whatever category and comparison set the positioning sets up. Get the category wrong (leading with "AI" instead of "Creative Director") and every subsequent marketing dollar/hour reaches the wrong evaluators with the wrong criteria.
- The **Big Fish, Small Pond** style — narrow, listable, underserved subsegment — is the correct default marketing frame for a solo operator; head-to-head against agencies or inventing a new category both require resources a solo practice doesn't have.
- **The "logo test"** (from `brain-audit.md`, applies equally to marketing copy): paste a competitor's name over your tagline — if it still reads true, it's not differentiated enough to market.

## Channel logic — the Core Four, prioritized for this business (Hormozi)

Warm outreach → referrals → narrow content → cold outreach → paid ads, in that order for a solo, reputation-dependent, small-market operator. Paid ads require margin and volume proof this business doesn't yet have; content compounds but slowly; warm outreach and referrals are near-zero-cost and highest-conversion — they are underused precisely because they don't feel like "doing marketing." (See `lead-generation.md` for the full channel breakdown.)

## Message construction (D'Souza)

- Lead with the PROBLEM, specifically named, before any mention of your solution or capability. Marketing copy that opens with capability ("I design brand systems that convert") underperforms copy that opens with the client's exact pain.
- Every piece of marketing collateral (portfolio page, one-pager, case study) should pre-answer the seven objections: is this a real problem, does the solution work, is this for someone like me, what about [specific objection], can I trust the testimonials, what's the risk if it doesn't work, why this person and not a competitor.
- Testimonials built from the six-question method (ask live, by voice) — a generic "great to work with!" is marketing waste; a testimonial that names the specific pre-project doubt and how it resolved is marketing collateral that does the objection-handling work for you.

## Building the one-page plan (worked skeleton, applied to a job search)

| Square | Draft answer |
|---|---|
| 1. Target market | Your target archetypes from `modes/_profile.md` — the specific companies/roles you're optimizing for, not "anyone hiring" |
| 2. Message | Your headline + exit narrative from `config/profile.yml` — the one-line version of why you're a fit |
| 3. Media | LinkedIn, portfolio/case-study site, warm referrals, the boards and ATSs configured in `portals.yml` |
| 4. Lead capture | A live portfolio/case-study deck a recruiter can skim in under 2 minutes |
| 5. Lead nurture | `data/follow-ups.md` cadence |
| 6. Conversion | `sales-conversations.md` (discovery framing applied to interviews) → `modes/negotiation.md` (comp close) |

Dib's original canvas continues with deliver/retain/refer squares (7-9) for an ongoing
client relationship — less directly applicable to a one-time hire, though the same
"ask a specific person at the appreciation moment" logic applies to asking a new
manager or teammate for a LinkedIn recommendation once you've landed.

## Cautions

- Dib's SMB tactics (urgency language, broad discount offers, mass lead magnets) can undercut premium creative-director positioning if applied literally — use the CANVAS structure, not the SMB tone.
- Marketing systems compound slowly; don't judge square 3 (media/content) against square 4 (direct lead capture) on the same timeline — content is a multi-quarter bet, warm outreach is a multi-week one.
- A niche that scores well on PVP but has too small a prospect pool (Baker's test, `business-of-expertise.md`) isn't a niche — widen geography or buyer type before committing marketing spend to it.
